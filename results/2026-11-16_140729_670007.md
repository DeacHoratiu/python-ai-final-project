# **Who Were the Minoans?** 

A Bronze Age civilization on the island of Crete 

The Minoans were the people of a remarkable Bronze Age culture centered on Crete, the large island south of mainland Greece. Their civilization grew during the third millennium BCE and became especially prosperous between roughly 1900 and 1450 BCE. It is famous for complex palaces, elegant art, overseas trade, and early writing. 

The name “Minoan” is modern. Archaeologist Arthur Evans used it after the legendary King Minos, who appears in Greek stories about Crete and the Minotaur. We do not know what the people called themselves. Nor can we read most of their surviving texts, so archaeology gives us much of their history.

Records that historians still cannot fully read 

The Minoans used several forms of writing, including Cretan Hieroglyphic and Linear A. The signs appear on clay tablets, sealings, pottery, and ritual objects. Many surviving documents were connected with keeping track of goods and administrative activities.
